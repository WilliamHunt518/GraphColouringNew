Two Tiers, Two Scenarios

Every session collected so far, pooled instead of profiled one participant at a time. The question here is not who did well — it is whether operators treat the Strategic Assistant and the Tactical Assistant differently, and whether that changes between the Strategic Heavy and Tactical Heavy scenarios. Because the build moved underneath the data several times, every figure is scoped to a build window you choose.

Build window — every figure below is scoped to this selection

Question one

Do operators treat the two assistants differently?

Both tiers offer the same bargain — take what the assistant proposes, or build it yourself. Uptake asks whether the assistant was invited into the decision at all. Acceptance asks whether what it proposed then went out unchanged. They are not the same question, and the data separates them sharply.

Question two

Does the scenario change the bargain?

Strategic Heavy sends many small missions; Tactical Heavy sends fewer, larger ones. The headless harness calibrates both to roughly equal achievability. Operators do not experience them as equal.

Question two, controlled

Each participant against themselves

Every participant ran both scenarios, so their own pair controls for skill — the comparison that survives a sample this small. Each line is one person moving from their Strategic Heavy session to their Tactical Heavy one.

Question three

Does disposition predict which assistant you lean on?

Three things were measured per person: how they feel about AI in general (before they ever saw the app), how much they trusted each assistant (after every session), and how often they actually used each one. The pre-study battery only exists on builds from study-v1.3 onward, which is the binding constraint on everything in this section.
